# Jump, massé, and combo drills in the catalogue

The shots that win crowded tables are often the ones people refuse to practice: **jumps**, **massé**, **combos**, plus the banks and kicks that set them up. Highlight-reel luck is usually reps.

**[Shots](/shots)** is the RackUp catalogue — diagrammed drills you can open on the phone at the table. **[Coach](/coach)** is the daily door into that library (Shot of the Day, drills, Coach AI). Together they are the practice side of the Rack of Champions network.

## What the catalogue is for

- **Combos** — see the line through both balls, not just the hope.
- **Jumps** — airborne path over a blocker, not a scoop you will get called on.
- **Massé** — curve with a cue elevation you can repeat, not a prayer spin.
- **Banks and kicks** — the rails you needed anyway to make the pretty shot legal.

Each card is a drill, not a poster. Open it, rack it, hit it. The diagram should match the table in front of you closely enough that you are not guessing which ball is which.

## How to practice without wasting the hour

1. Pick one family (combo *or* jump *or* massé). Mixing all three is how you remember none.
2. Open **[Shots](/shots)** and run a handful in that family.
3. If today’s **[Coach](/coach)** Shot of the Day is in the same family, do that first — it is already chosen.
4. When a miss has a reason you cannot see, send it to Coach AI with a note or a clip.
5. Stop while you can still make the last three. Grinding to slop teaches slop.

## Take it to a live table

Practice that never meets an opponent is still useful — and incomplete.

- **[Find](/find)** someone who will let you play, not only watch you jump the 9.
- **[Halls](/halls)** so you are on a table that exists.
- **[Play](/play)** if the set has a stake and you want dual confirm.
- **[Tournaments](/tournaments)** if the house is bracketing and you need those shots under a clock.

The catalogue does not care about your rating. The looking board will.
